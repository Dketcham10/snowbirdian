Scottsdale short-term rental license guide

The steps an owner usually takes before hosting stays of 31 nights or fewer in Scottsdale. Official city and state pages are linked below so fee and deadline details stay current.

Scottsdale requires short-term and vacation rental owners to complete city licensing in addition to Arizona tax registration. This page is a checklist of the common steps, not legal advice. Rules, fees, and forms change. Always confirm details on the official City of Scottsdale and Arizona Department of Revenue pages linked here.

1. Arizona Transaction Privilege Tax (TPT) license

Short-term lodging income is subject to Arizona transaction privilege tax. Owners typically register for a TPT license with the Arizona Department of Revenue before applying for the city license. Scottsdale’s owner guidance asks you to list Scottsdale as a region code on that license.

  • Arizona Department of Revenue: Short-Term Lodging
  • AZTaxes.gov registration and filing

2. City of Scottsdale short-term rental license

For each Scottsdale property offered for short stays, the city requires an annual short-term / vacation rental license. The city’s owner page explains the process, including account setup and the documents you will need. Your TPT number is part of that application.

  • City of Scottsdale: Information for Owners and Operators
  • City of Scottsdale: Vacation and Short-Term Rentals

3. Emergency contact and neighbor notice

Scottsdale’s licensing process includes emergency contact information for the property and neighbor notification requirements. The city publishes attestation and neighbor-notice materials on the same vacation rental pages. Use those official forms and instructions rather than a third-party summary when you file or update contacts.

If you use a local host or manager, make sure the emergency contact listed with the city is someone who can actually respond. That detail matters to neighbors and to the city as much as it matters to guests.

Keep records as you go

Save confirmations from AZTaxes, your Scottsdale license approval, and a copy of whatever neighbor notice or attestation the city asks for at the time you file. When emergency contacts change, update the city and your listing platforms together so guests, neighbors, and the license file all point to the same person.
